Do I need a permit for a shed in Raleigh, Cary & Wake County?

Short answer: it depends on where you live and how big the shed is. Raleigh requires a permit for a shed of any size — a zoning permit for a small one, a building permit for a big one. Cary and Wake County require a building permit only when any dimension exceeds 12 feet — but in Wake County a small shed may still need a Land Use Permit, and Cary's rules don't say whether a small shed needs a zoning permit. The line that matters everywhere is 12 feet in any dimension.

Short answer: Raleigh yes at any size, Cary and Wake County only over 12 feet

All three jurisdictions say so in their own words:

  • Raleigh: “A permit is required regardless of the size of an accessory structure. You will receive either a zoning permit if under 12 feet in any direction (length, width, or height) or a building permit if an accessory structure exceeds 12 feet in any direction (length, width, or height).”
  • Cary: “sheds & enclosed buildings greater than 12 feet in any dimension, including tree houses” need a permit — and “Residential accessory buildings (not structures) that are no more than 12 feet in any dimension” do not.
  • Wake County: “Accessory buildings with any dimension greater than 12' require a building permit. Accessory buildings that DO NOT have a dimension greater than 12' may still require a Land Use Permit from Wake County Planning & Zoning.”

The 12-foot rule: the line is 12 feet in any dimension — length, width, or height. A long, narrow shed 10 feet wide and 16 feet long crosses it on length even though it is only 160 square feet.

Don't confuse the two kinds of “no permit”: North Carolina law exempts a one-story storage building no more than 12 feet in any dimension, and not on a permanent foundation, from the building-code permit. That is not the same as no permit at all — Raleigh still requires a zoning permit for a small shed, and Wake County may still require a Land Use Permit.

What needs a permit vs. what doesn't

Needs a permit:

  • Any shed in Raleigh, of any size — a zoning permit under 12 feet in any dimension, a building permit over it
  • A shed over 12 feet in any dimension in Cary and Wake County — a building permit
  • A shed with wiring, lights, or outlets — a separate electrical permit, in addition to the building or zoning permit

Usually does NOT need a building permit:

  • A shed no more than 12 feet in every dimension, in Cary and Wake County

But “no building permit” is not the whole story for a small shed: in Wake County it may still require a Land Use Permit from Planning & Zoning, and Cary's FAQ does not say whether a small shed needs a zoning permit. Ask the jurisdiction before you build — that question is free and the wrong guess is not.

Raleigh (inside Raleigh city limits)

Who handles it: The City of Raleigh Planning and Development Department, through its Permit and Development Portal.

The rule: A permit is required regardless of the size of an accessory structure. Under 12 feet in any direction (length, width, or height) you receive a zoning permit; over 12 feet in any direction you receive a building permit. Raleigh's rule covers sheds, detached garages, carports, greenhouses, pool houses, gazebos, and workshops — anything classed as an accessory structure.

How to apply: Accessory structures over 12 feet in any direction apply through the Permit and Development Portal. Structures under 12 feet apply by email, sending the completed application and supporting documents to the Development Services team.

What to submit: A completed residential permit application, a site plan indicating the proposed work, elevation drawings, and structural plans. A Residential Permit Data Form is required if the structure is greater than 15 feet in height. If you are a homeowner acting as the contractor, an Owner Exemption Affidavit is required.

Inspections: Raleigh states that when inspections are required, city inspectors check the work at key stages before the project is completed. Your permit documents will list the required inspections.

Cary (inside Cary town limits)

Who handles it: The Town of Cary Inspections & Permits Department (Cary Town Hall, 316 North Academy Street; 311 or (919) 469-4000). Apply online through the Town's electronic plan review portal.

The rule, stated plainly: A building permit is required for sheds and enclosed buildings greater than 12 feet in any dimension, including tree houses. A residential accessory building no more than 12 feet in any dimension needs no building permit.

The open question: Cary's FAQ does not say whether a small shed needs a zoning or land-use permit. The building permit is clearly not required under 12 feet; the zoning position is unstated. Check with the Town (311 or (919) 469-4000) before you build.

Plot plan: Any time the footprint of an accessory building changes, a proposed plot plan and an as-built survey are required — Cary lists storage buildings explicitly. The Town accepts existing surveys and hand-drawn site plans.

Faster review: Cary's expedited SPOT review covers storage buildings under 500 square feet. A plans examiner will say if a project is too complex for SPOT review.

Fees: Check Cary's fee schedule for the current amounts.

Wake County (unincorporated areas)

Who handles it — and how to tell: Wake County Inspections & Permits provides building inspections and permitting for all unincorporated areas of Wake County, and, through contractual agreements, inspection services for Knightdale, Rolesville, Wendell, and Zebulon. If you live inside one of those four towns, you apply through the town and Wake County provides the inspections. If you are unsure of your jurisdiction, look your property up on Wake County's iMaps site: if “WC” is listed as your planning jurisdiction, you are in Wake County's jurisdiction. Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Wake Forest, and other municipalities run their own departments and are not covered here.

The rule: Accessory buildings with any dimension greater than 12 feet require a building permit. Buildings that do not have a dimension greater than 12 feet may still require a Land Use Permit from Wake County Planning & Zoning — ask before you assume a small shed is permit-free.

What to submit (Wake County Permit Portal):

  1. A completed application using the Wake County Permit Portal.
  2. A site plan meeting the County's specifications.
  3. Building plans meeting the County's specifications.
  4. A certificate of workers' compensation insurance, or a completed affidavit of workers' compensation exemption, for contractors.

Extra reviews that may apply:

  • Watershed management: If there are flood hazard areas or your proposed impervious area exceeds the allowable limit, a watershed review may apply — check the County's current fee schedule.
  • Septic/well: If the property is served by an onsite wastewater system, the shed's location must be checked so it does not impact the system or its repair area, and maintains the required setbacks from the system and any on-site wells.

Fees and licensing notes: If the project costs $40,000 or more and the owner is listed as the general contractor, a notarized owner-contractor affidavit is required. Check the County's current fee schedule for permit amounts.

Inspections: Schedule through the Wake County Permit Portal. To get a next-business-day inspection, submit the request before 3:00 p.m. on the previous business day. Your permit documents will list the required inspections.

What happens if you build without one

Raleigh's own residential permits page states the consequences plainly, and they are the right mental model for all three jurisdictions:

  • A citation from the city
  • Fines, increased permit fees, or possible court costs
  • Safety hazards in your home or on your property
  • Having to remove or redo work at the property owner's expense
  • Delays or complications when selling your home or property if unpermitted work is discovered
  • Insurance coverage issues related to unpermitted work

The cheapest version of this story is always the same: get the permit first, while the work is still visible and the fix is still cheap.

HOA approval is separate from a permit

A city or county permit and your homeowners association's approval are two different things. A permit does not replace HOA architectural review, and HOA approval does not replace a permit — if your neighborhood has an HOA, expect to need both, and check your HOA's documents early, since HOA review runs on its own timeline.

Verify before you build

Permit rules change, and the details that matter — setbacks, floodplain and watershed reviews, septic clearances, historic-district approvals — are specific to your lot. Before you build, confirm your jurisdiction (Cary, Raleigh, or Wake County — see the iMaps check above for unincorporated Wake) and confirm the current requirements with that jurisdiction's office. Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit for a small shed?

In Raleigh, yes — a permit is required regardless of the size of an accessory structure, a zoning permit for a shed under 12 feet in any dimension and a building permit above it. In Cary and Wake County, a shed no more than 12 feet in every dimension needs no building permit. In Wake County a small shed may still require a Land Use Permit from Planning & Zoning, and Cary's FAQ does not say whether a small shed needs a zoning permit — check with the town first.

What does “12 feet in any dimension” mean?

Length, width, or height — whichever is longest. A long, narrow shed 10 feet wide and 16 feet long crosses the line on length, even though it is only 160 square feet. If any one dimension exceeds 12 feet, the building permit is required in all three jurisdictions.

Does a shed on skids or without a foundation need a permit?

Not a building permit, in the narrow case North Carolina law describes: a one-story storage building no more than 12 feet in any dimension and not on a permanent foundation is exempt from the building-code permit. But that exemption is not the same as no permit at all — Raleigh still requires a zoning permit for a small shed, and Wake County may still require a Land Use Permit.

Does a shed with electricity need a separate permit?

Yes. Wiring, lights, or outlets in a shed are electrical work and need their own electrical permit — the building permit does not cover the wiring. Cary's FAQ lists adding lighting and receptacle outlets as permit-required electrical work.

Do I need a plot plan or site plan for a shed?

Yes, in all three. Raleigh requires a site plan with the application. Cary requires a plot plan any time the footprint of an accessory building changes, and the town lists storage buildings explicitly. Wake County requires a site plan meeting its specifications, plus building plans.

Can a small shed get a faster review in Cary?

Yes — Cary's expedited SPOT review covers storage buildings under 500 square feet, along with decks, porches, and additions under 500 square feet. A plans examiner will say if a project is too complex for SPOT review.

What if my shed is over 12 feet tall but small on the ground?

Height counts. The 12-foot line applies to any dimension, including height, so a tall, narrow shed over 12 feet high needs a building permit in all three jurisdictions — a zoning permit is not enough, and in Raleigh the application goes through the Permit Portal rather than by email.
